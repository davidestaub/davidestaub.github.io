HD 189733 b

Visible-light secondary eclipses constrain reflected colour. JWST transmission spectra identify molecular absorption as starlight passes through the atmosphere.

A blue colour is supported. Exact clouds, their geography and glass rain are not directly measured. Keep the famous sideways-glass-rain story out of the main copy.

WASP-43 b

JWST followed the planet through a full orbit. Its infrared brightness corresponds to about 1,250 °C on the day side and 590 °C on the night side in the measured wavelength range. Models with thick night-side clouds fit the faint night-time emission. The clouds block light from deeper, hotter layers. They can also trap heat, so faint emission does not mean the clouds cool the whole atmosphere. The climate models assume synchronous rotation.

Brightness temperatures describe the emitting atmosphere in a wavelength band. They are not ground temperatures. Exact cloud shapes are not mapped.

  1. Nightside clouds and disequilibrium chemistry on the hot Jupiter WASP-43b
  2. Latitudinal Asymmetry in the Dayside Atmosphere of WASP-43b
  3. The radiative and dynamical impact of clouds in the atmosphere of the hot Jupiter WASP-43 b

WASP-17 b

JWST measured an infrared feature near 8.6 micrometres. Models with tiny quartz particles reproduce its shape. Grant et al. adopt a radius of 1.932 Jupiter radii and a mass of 0.477 Jupiter masses, the consistent pair used for the introduction. The archive table below preserves its separately sourced values.

The quartz identification comes from cloud models fitted to the spectrum. Particle size depends on the model. The introduction uses the mass and radius adopted together in Grant et al. (2023), rather than combining them with the different archive mass. Resolve the catalogue parameter choice before publication.

System

One star and its confirmed planets, drawn to scale from the catalogue and flown through from a ship. Sizes and orbits are true to the numbers; nobody knows what any of these planets looks like, so every surface, colour and cloud is an assumption and is listed as one in the dossier on the cockpit.

Notes

What is measured. The star's radius, temperature and mass, and each planet's radius, mass, orbital period, semi-major axis and eccentricity come from the catalogue where they exist. Where one of a pair is missing it is derived from the other and marked so. The scene is at true scale: one scene unit is 1,000 km, so a hot Jupiter sits a few stellar radii out and a one-year orbit is a large empty circle. The background stars are the bright-star catalogue moved to the host's position, and the minimap places every host at its catalogued distance and direction.

What is imagined. No exoplanet has ever been resolved as a disc. Surface pattern, colour, cloud cover, spin, the orientation of the orbit and the starting position along it are all chosen by the renderer from rules based on the planet's class and equilibrium temperature. Those rules are stated in the dossier's "imagined" column; treat them as an illustration, not as a prediction.

What the speeds mean. The speed readout is true: kilometres per second through a scene at true scale. To cross a system in minutes the ship has to go far faster than light, and when it does the readout says so as a multiple of c. That is a convenience of the game, not physics. A warp between systems is the same fiction, and its readout states how many years light would need for the same trip.

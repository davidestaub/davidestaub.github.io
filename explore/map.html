The known planets, on the map

Every confirmed exoplanet with a measured distance, drawn at its catalogued position in the Galaxy. Distances are the system distances compiled by the NASA Exoplanet Archive, mostly Gaia parallaxes; the microlensing planets toward the bulge carry model-based lens distances with large uncertainties. The backdrop is an artist's concept of the Milky Way seen from above the north galactic pole, calibrated so the Sun and the Galactic Centre sit where they should. Each point is a host star, coloured by its temperature. Click a star, then "Fly there" to open its system in the cockpit.

    Notes

    What is real. Every point is a confirmed planet host with a catalogued distance, placed by converting its sky position and distance into galactic coordinates. The scale rings are centred on the Sun at 100, 1,000 and 10,000 light-years. The spike of stars in one direction is the Kepler field, where thousands of planets were found along a single line of sight. The chain of points running from the Sun toward the Galactic Centre is the microlensing sample; their distances are model estimates, not parallaxes.

    What is a model. The backdrop is an artist's concept of the Milky Way, not a photograph. It is calibrated so that the marked Sun sits 8,178 parsecs from the Galactic Centre and the galactic longitude directions match, but the spiral arms are illustrative. Point colours are a blackbody approximation from the host star's effective temperature; hosts without a temperature are drawn in neutral white.
